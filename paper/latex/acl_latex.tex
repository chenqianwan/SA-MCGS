\begin{abstract}
Large Language Models (LLMs) achieve strong performance on many NLP tasks
but remain fundamentally limited as \emph{System-1} reasoners: they rely on
single-pass inference over linearized token sequences and fail when tasks
require tracking cyclic dependencies across hundreds of interconnected
elements. We propose \textbf{SA-MCGS} (\textbf{S}tructure-\textbf{A}ware
\textbf{M}onte \textbf{C}arlo \textbf{G}raph \textbf{S}earch), a framework
that augments LLM reasoning with \emph{System-2} capabilities by decomposing
analytical tasks into directed dependency graphs, identifying reasoning
bottlenecks via Strongly Connected Component (SCC) detection, and applying
AlphaGo-inspired Monte Carlo search to systematically explore hypothesis
spaces within these bottlenecks. An Online Conformal Prediction layer
provides distribution-free reliability guarantees. We evaluate SA-MCGS on
legal contract analysis using the CUAD and CLAUSE benchmarks across 311
experimental runs. SA-MCGS achieves \textbf{100\%} detection of structural
anomalies within SCC scope (vs.\ 47\% for Gemini-2.5), exhibits graceful
degradation across five difficulty tiers (100\%$\to$80\%), and maintains
stable performance at 500+ interdependent clauses where pure LLM reasoning
collapses beyond 60--80. We further identify a novel \emph{Magnifier Effect}
where the search process itself encodes diagnostic signals invisible to
single-pass inference.

% --- 2.1 Reasoning Gap ---
\subsection{The Reasoning Gap in LLMs}
\label{sec:rw_reasoning}

Despite breakthroughs in language understanding, LLMs exhibit well-documented failures in structured reasoning. The ``Lost in the Middle'' phenomenon \citep{liu2024lost} demonstrates that transformer attention degrades for information positioned away from document boundaries, with retrieval accuracy dropping by up to 20\% for mid-context information. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting \citep{wei2022chain} partially addresses sequential reasoning but fundamentally cannot handle branching or cyclic logic.

Benchmarks in the legal domain have quantified these limitations. CUAD \citep{hendrycks2021cuad} demonstrated that even fine-tuned models struggle with cross-clause consistency. ContractNLI \citep{koreeda2021contractnli} showed that document-level inference remains challenging when evidence spans non-contiguous sections. The CLAUSE benchmark \citep{clause2026} provides the first systematic evaluation of structural legal reasoning, revealing that Gemini-2.5 achieves only 46.7\% F1 on structural flaws and LLaMA-3.3 drops to 14.7\%.

% --- 2.2 Search-Augmented Reasoning ---
\subsection{Search-Augmented LLM Reasoning}
\label{sec:rw_search}

Tree of Thoughts \citep{yao2024tree} extends CoT by exploring multiple reasoning paths via BFS/DFS, but its tree structure cannot represent merging or cyclic reasoning patterns. Graph of Thoughts \citep{besta2024graph} generalizes to graph-structured reasoning with thought merging and refinement, but operates on LLM-generated thought graphs rather than externally constructed dependency graphs, making it susceptible to the same hallucination problems it aims to solve. Reasoning via Planning (RAP) \citep{hao2023reasoning} applies MCTS directly to LLM reasoning chains, treating each step as a game move. RAP is closest to our work but operates in the space of reasoning steps rather than document structure, lacks topological focusing (SCC detection), and does not address cyclic dependencies.

SA-MCGS advances this line by operating on \emph{externally constructed} dependency graphs and introducing topological analysis to focus search on critical reasoning bottlenecks.

% --- 2.3 MCTS Beyond Games ---
\subsection{Monte Carlo Tree Search Beyond Games}
\label{sec:rw_mcts}

MCTS has been the backbone of modern AI achievements in games \citep{silver2016mastering,silver2017mastering}. The UCT algorithm's convergence proof \citep{kocsis2006bandit} fundamentally requires finite search depth ($D < \infty$). \citet{czech2021monte} extended MCTS to Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) for AlphaZero, enabling transposition sharing, but \emph{explicitly} restricts the search structure to be acyclic. \citet{moerland2020mcts} identified that loops cause $O(2^N)$ complexity explosion in MCTS and proposed trace-level loop blocking (MCTS-T+), but this solution discards all information within loops.

SA-MCGS addresses this gap: it detects SCCs via Tarjan's algorithm, performs information-preserving evaluation within cycles, and collapses them into DAG super-nodes, restoring the finite-depth assumption required by UCT convergence theory.

% --- 2.4 Contract Graph Analysis ---
\subsection{Contract Graph Construction and Analysis}
\label{sec:rw_graph}

GRAPH-GRPO-LEX \citep{dechtiar2025graphgrpolex} uses GRPO reinforcement learning to train LLMs for automated contract graph extraction, achieving 0.798 strict micro-F1. Their cycle detection module identifies logical flaws, but treats cycles solely as defect signals without further search or risk quantification on SCCs. \citet{boulet2021measuring} analyze cross-reference graphs of the US Code and German federal law via QuantLaw, finding that SCCs constitute $\sim$15\% of legal networks, but perform only descriptive measurement without risk assessment.

Our work is complementary: GRAPH-GRPO-LEX solves graph \emph{construction}; SA-MCGS solves \emph{search and risk evaluation} on the constructed graph.

% --- 2.5 Conformal Prediction ---
\subsection{Conformal Prediction for AI Reliability}
\label{sec:rw_conformal}

Conformal prediction provides distribution-free statistical guarantees for prediction uncertainty \citep{vovk2005algorithmic}. Online variants \citep{gibbs2021adaptive} adapt to non-stationary data streams, making them suitable for the sequential nature of search-based evaluation. We leverage Online Conformal Prediction to transform stochastic LLM rollout outputs into calibrated risk assessments with mathematically grounded confidence levels.
