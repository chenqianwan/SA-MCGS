\title{Risk Subgraph Extraction in Cyclic Document Graphs\\
with Structure-Aware Monte Carlo Search}

\author{Anonymous ARR Submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Long documents such as contracts, statutes, software package metadata, and corporate filings often contain risks that emerge through mutually dependent records.  LLM-guided Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) can improve structured document reasoning, but standard tree rollouts become unstable on strongly connected components (SCCs), where the same logical record may be revisited indefinitely.  We propose \samcgs{}, a structure-aware Monte Carlo graph search method that localizes SCCs, performs local LLM rollouts with relation-first evidence memory, and collapses each cyclic region into an inspectable risk subgraph.  We evaluate a locked critical structural benchmark covering four authority-backed domains: Debian dependencies, SEC EX-21 subsidiary disclosures, German Civil Code cross-references, and CUAD contracts.  Across five LLMs, \samcgs{} raises mean \rootthree{} and \riskall{} over local evidence aggregation (LEA) by 6.75 and 15.25 percentage points, respectively. The corresponding gains over one-shot prompting are 46.50 and 39.00 points. Returned subgraphs remove 55.70\% of SCC nodes on average. \revreplace{A blinded expert audit}{Expert evaluation} further supports the quality of the extracted evidence. Together, these results show that \samcgs{} improves risk localization and evidence completeness while producing compact subgraphs for human inspection.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Many high-stakes document analysis tasks are graph problems hidden inside text \citep{hendrycks2021cuad,koreeda2021contractnli,boulet2021measuring}.  In contracts, one clause may define a deadline, another may suspend it, and a later amendment may restore only part of the obligation.  Similar dependencies appear when statutory exceptions are narrowed by distant cross-references \citep{boulet2021measuring,holzenberger2020sara}, or when software packages preserve old interfaces while dependent packages require newer ones to be present first \citep{debianPolicyRelationships,debianPolicySharedLibraries}.  Although each record can look plausible in isolation, the risk appears only when the directed dependencies among records are followed.

Large language models (LLMs) can read these records in a single context, but context length alone does not ensure reliable retrieval, multi-hop tracing, or evidence aggregation \citep{liu2024lost,hsieh2024ruler,bai2024longbench}. Following dependencies within strongly connected components (\scc{}s) can revisit the same records. One-shot prompting must jointly rank risk sources and assemble supporting evidence from the entire component in one generation. Complete evidence retention is therefore a distinct objective from locating a suspicious record.

MCTS supports game playing \citep{silver2016mastering} and LLM reasoning \citep{zhou2024lats,song2025rekgmcts}.  Standard MCTS unrolls trajectories as trees.  In a loopy graph, this makes the search state grow with
walk prefixes instead of physical records.  In an \scc{}, even a
small set of records can therefore induce many repeated path copies. Related MCTS analyses describe this as a transposition, graph-history, and loop-induced uncertainty
problem \citep{childs2008transpositions,kishimoto2004general,saffidine2012ucd,moerland2020mcts}.
Prior Monte Carlo graph search shares transpositions in acyclic search graphs \citep{czech2021monte}, while feedback-edge methods obtain directed acyclic graph (DAG) approximations by removing or reorienting edges \citep{eades1993fast,berger1990approximation}. Risk extraction requires a search representation that preserves cyclic relations while consolidating evidence about the same physical records.

We propose \textbf{\samcgs{}}, a structure-aware Monte Carlo graph search method that performs local LLM rollouts within each \scc{}. A record\textquotesingle{}s priority depends on how its constraints, exceptions, or handoffs interact with accumulated evidence. Relation-first memory preserves this context across rollouts and guides exploration of implicated records and relations. \revreplace{Evidence-guided}{After search, evidence-guided} core selection integrates accumulated node and relation evidence into a compact risk subgraph for inspection (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}).

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
    \item A formulation of \emph{structural risk subgraph extraction}: retaining repair-relevant evidence in compact subgraphs of cyclic document regions.
    \item \samcgs{}, an adaptive search method combining upper confidence bound (UCB) selection with progressive relation priors, critical-pair revisiting, and evidence-gated core pruning.
    \item A fixed four-domain benchmark comparing \samcgs{} with one-shot prompting and LEA across five LLMs, with component ablations, inference-cost measurements, and expert evaluation. \samcgs{} achieves higher mean \rootthree{} and \riskall{} than either baseline.
\end{itemize}

\section{Task Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

\paragraph{Record graph.}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a directed graph whose nodes are document records and whose edges denote typed dependencies such as reference, constraint, modification, trigger, or control.  A record may be a clause, statute section, package metadata entry, subsidiary disclosure, or other domain unit.  We use Tarjan's algorithm \citep{tarjan1972depth} to decompose $G$ into strongly connected components.  \revreplace{A component $S\subseteq V$ is a reasoning bottleneck when every node in $S$ can reach every other node through directed dependencies.}{An SCC has node set $S\subseteq V$ and induced graph $G_S=(S,E_S)$, with every node reachable from every other node.}

\paragraph{Structural risk.}
We study relational risks whose evidence extends beyond suspicious wording.    The core evidence contains a root record, a witness record, and sometimes affected records.  The root and witness can each be locally plausible, but jointly create an incompatible obligation, temporal condition, ownership statement, or dependency handoff.

\paragraph{Output.}
\revreplace{Given a strongly connected component $S$, the system returns a risk subgraph $H\subseteq S$ and, when applicable, a ranking over risky records.}{Given $G_S$, the system returns a node set $H\subseteq S$, identified with its induced risk subgraph, and a record ranking.}  We evaluate:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{\rootthree{}}: whether the injected root appears in the top three ranked records.
    \item \textbf{\riskany{}}: whether $H$ contains at least one repair-relevant risk endpoint.
    \item \textbf{\riskall{}}: whether $H$ retains all core risk endpoints.  This criterion is strict since remediation often requires seeing both sides of the contradiction.
    \item \textbf{Compression}: $1-|H|/|S|$, where higher values indicate smaller subgraphs.  Compression is not optimized alone; a tiny subgraph that drops the evidence endpoints is not useful.
\end{itemize}
We report effective compression and effective \oc{} as diagnostics in Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics}; they are not primary success metrics.
An unmodified component is a source \scc{}; each controlled edit produces an injected variant. Evaluating one variant with one model constitutes a model-case. Variants from the same source are not independent samples. Expert evidence and repair-material coverage are evaluated separately from the benchmark endpoint metrics.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Why ordinary MCTS is not enough}
\label{sec:mcts_motivation}

Standard tree search treats a trajectory as a growing path, following the usual MCTS loop of selection, expansion, simulation, and backpropagation \citep{browne2012survey}.  In a DAG, this assumption is valid. Rollouts eventually terminate, and backpropagation updates a finite set of states.  In an \scc{}, the same physical record can be revisited through many path copies \citep{childs2008transpositions}.  If a rollout path is $p=(v_1,\ldots,v_\ell)$, ordinary TreeMCTS indexes states by path prefix.  A physical node $v$ can therefore appear as many distinct tree states,
\ifrevisionmarks
\[\revdeletedmath{\mathcal P_T(v)=\{p_{\leq t}:p_t=v,\;p\in\Pi_T\},}\]
\fi
\begin{revadded}
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal P_T(v)=\{p_{\leq j}:p_j=v,\;p\in\Pi_T\},
\end{equation}
\end{revadded}
\revreplace{where $\Pi_T$ is the set of paths generated before budget $T$.}{where $j$ indexes positions along $p$ and $\Pi_T$ contains paths generated before budget $T$.}  With this indexing, path multiplicity governs the effective state space in place of $|S|$.  Repeated revisits then consume rollout budget, increase truncation, and dilute value estimates.

\revreplace{Figure~\ref{fig:mcts_failure} contrasts path-indexed TreeMCTS with \scc{}-localized search. With matched rollout budgets,}{Figure~\ref{fig:mcts_failure} compares four mechanisms. In the matched-budget TreeMCTS--\samcgs{} diagnostic (Table~\ref{tab:mcts_scc}),} TreeMCTS expands 6.4 times as many states as physical nodes in the cyclic setting, and all rollouts are truncated. \samcgs{} limits expansion to 1.0 times the component size and increases Q-spread from 0.044 to 0.371. Table~\ref{tab:mcts_scc} includes the acyclic control; Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostics} defines the diagnostic setup and metrics.

\subsection{Objective}

\samcgs{} avoids path-copy states by treating \revreplace{each \scc{} $S$ as a localized graph object}{each induced SCC graph $G_S$ as a local search unit}.  The desired output is a subgraph $H\subseteq S$ that keeps enough evidence for remediation while remaining smaller than the original component.  Let $R^\star\subseteq S$ denote the latent repair-relevant endpoints in evaluation, e.g., root and witness records.  At inference $R^\star$ is unknown, so the algorithm estimates node and relation utility from rollout evidence.  The empirical objective is given by
\begin{equation}
    \max_{H\subseteq S} \; \widehat{R}(H) - \ifrevisionmarks\revdeletedmath{\lambda}\,\fi\revadd{\lambda_s} \frac{|H|}{|S|},
    \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{R}(H)$ estimates evidence retention and \revreplace{$\lambda$ controls compression pressure}{$\lambda_s$ controls compression pressure}.  The objective balances risk coverage, measured by \riskany{} and \riskall{}, against compression, reflecting the need for a small $H$ to retain repair-relevant evidence.

\subsection{SCC-aware local rollouts}

We first decompose the record graph using Tarjan's linear-time algorithm \citep{tarjan1972depth}.  DAG regions are handled by the ordinary upstream search, whereas each non-trivial \scc{} is processed by \samcgs{}.  \revreplace{At rollout $t$, the algorithm selects a seed $a_t\in S$ and constructs a local window}{Each rollout uses a window with capacity $w$:}
\ifrevisionmarks
\[
    \revdeletedmath{W_t = \{v\in S : \operatorname{dist}_{S}(a_t,v)\leq r\} \cup B_t,}
\]
\fi
\begin{revadded}
\begin{equation}
    W_t\subseteq S,\qquad |W_t|\le\min(w,|S|).
    \label{eq:window-capacity}
\end{equation}
\end{revadded}
\revreplace{where $r$ is a small graph radius and $B_t$ contains selected bridge or memory nodes.}{Windows use relation-biased frontier expansion from a UCB-selected seed or a scheduled endpoint probe (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_formula_details}).}  The LLM evaluator observes node text and visible directed edges restricted to $W_t$, excluding the remainder of the \scc{}.  \revreplace{It returns local node scores $y_t(v)\in[0,1]$, relation evidence $z_t(u,v)\in[0,1]$, a local risk subgraph $h_t$, and optional \oc{} evidence.}{A consumed entry $\mathcal E_t$ contains node scores $y_t(v)\in[0,1]$, structured pair reports, and a local subgraph $h_t$.}

Seed selection and frontier expansion follow the UCB intuition used in MCTS \citep{kocsis2006bandit}. Statistics are keyed by physical graph objects instead of path copies.  Let $x$ denote either a seed node $v$ or a frontier edge $e=(u,v)$.  \samcgs{} scores both with the same exploration template:
\ifrevisionmarks
\[
\begin{aligned}
    &\revdeletedmath{U_t(x)=\;\bar{r}_t(x)
    + c_t\sqrt{\frac{\log(1+N_t)}
    {n_t(x)+\ell_t(x)+\epsilon}}}\\
    &\revdeletedmath{+\frac{\lambda\pi_t(x)}
    {1+\delta\sqrt{n_t(x)}}+\eta_t(x),}
\end{aligned}
\]
\fi
\begin{revadded}
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    U(x)=&\;\bar r(x)
    + c\sqrt{\frac{\log(1+N)}
    {n(x)+\ell(x)+\epsilon}}\\
    &+\frac{\lambda_p\pi(x)}
    {1+\delta\sqrt{n(x)}}+\eta(x),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:ucb}
\end{equation}
\end{revadded}
\revreplace{where $\bar{r}_t(x)$ is the current empirical risk or conflict estimate, $n_t(x)$ is the number of times $x$ has been observed, $\ell_t(x)$ is virtual loss for concurrent workers, $\pi_t(x)$ is a relation-first prior, and $\eta_t(x)$ is exploration noise.}{All quantities use the selection-time snapshot: $\bar r(v)=\bar q(v)$ for nodes, whereas edges use mean conflict indicators; $n$ counts visits, $\ell$ is weighted virtual loss, $\pi$ is the relation prior, $\lambda_p$ its weight, and $\eta$ exploration noise (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_formula_details}).}  \revreplace{The prior is computed from rollout evidence without domain-specific labels.}{Its normalized base component uses rollout evidence without domain-specific labels:}
\ifrevisionmarks
\[
    \revdeletedmath{\pi_t(x)=\frac{[w^\top\phi_t(x)]_+}{\max_{x'}[w^\top\phi_t(x')]_+ + \epsilon}.}
\]
\fi
\begin{revadded}
\begin{equation}
    \pi_t^0(x)=
    \frac{[\mathbf w^\top\phi_t(x)]_+}
    {\max_{x'}[\mathbf w^\top\phi_t(x')]_+ + \epsilon}.
    \label{eq:relation-prior}
\end{equation}
\end{revadded}
\revadd{Frontier edge priors also incorporate the entering node's prior, capped at one; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_formula_details} gives the exact rule.}
For nodes, $\phi_t(x)$ contains repair-entry counts, local risk-subgraph appearances, conflict-pair endpoint support, AMAF/RAVE-style evidence, path-fragment support, and \revreplace{\oc{} endpoint indicators}{\oc{} node flags}.  For edges, it contains \revreplace{semantic incompatibility}{critical-pair scores, semantic strength}, repeated pair support, explicit conflict reports, reverse-edge support, and accumulated conflict evidence.  Weights \revreplace{$w$}{$\mathbf w$} are fixed before the locked evaluation and reported in Appendix~\ref{app:priors}.  The decay term in Eq.~\ref{eq:ucb} prevents an early prior from dominating after sufficient node or edge samples.  Together, these choices implement progressive relation bias, analogous in spirit to progressive bias and RAVE-style reuse \citep{gelly2007combining,chaslot2008progressive,browne2012survey}, while keeping the update key tied to physical nodes and edges as graph-level objects \citep{czech2021monte}.

\subsection{Relation-first evidence memory}

Structural risk is relational in the sense that a node can appear harmless until previous evidence makes it incompatible with the graph context.  This matches the relational-inductive-bias view that graph-structured reasoning should represent entities together with the relations that define their roles \citep{battaglia2018relational}.  We therefore maintain memory
\ifrevisionmarks
\[\revdeletedmath{M_t = \{n_t(v),\bar{q}_t(v),m_t(u,v),o_t(v)\}_{v,u\in S},}\]
\fi
\begin{revadded}
\begin{equation}
    M_t = \{n_t(v),\bar{q}_t(v),m_t(u,v),o_t(v),\ldots\}_{v,u\in S},
\end{equation}
\end{revadded}
\revreplace{where $m_t(u,v)$ stores support that the directed relation $(u,v)$ makes either endpoint risky, and $o_t(v)$ stores whether $v$ has appeared in effective \oc{} evidence. Node evidence is updated by running averages and relation evidence by smoothed pair support. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_formula_details} gives the exact update equations.}{Here $M_t$ is the memory after consuming entry $\mathcal E_t$. For distinct nodes, $m_t(u,v)=m_t(v,u)=m_t(\{u,v\})$ counts unordered pair support; $o_t(v)=\mathbf1[v\in O_t]$ is the \oc{} flag. Node risk $\bar q_t(v)$ is the visit-counted mean. Pair memory also retains explicit-report counts, relation reasons, and a critical-pair ledger; directed edges keep separate exploration statistics (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_formula_details}).}  \revreplace{Let $r^-_t(v)$ and $r^+_t(v)$ be the strongest incoming and outgoing relation-memory scores around $v$.  The priority score used for core construction combines node evidence, relation evidence, \oc{} support, and a persistent critical-pair ledger:}{At the end of search, core priority combines accumulated evidence, the strongest qualifying pair support, and an \oc{} flag bonus:}
\ifrevisionmarks
\[
    \begin{aligned}
    &\revdeletedmath{s_t(v)=\bar{q}_t(v)+\alpha r^-_t(v)+\alpha r^+_t(v)}\\
    &\revdeletedmath{\quad+\gamma o_t(v)+\mu p_t(v).}
    \end{aligned}
\]
\fi
\begin{revadded}
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    b_T(v)&=\max_{p\in\mathcal{R}_T(v)}r_T(p),\\
    s_T(v)&=e_T(v)+\alpha b_T(v)+\gamma\mathbf{1}[v\in O_T].
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:priority}
\end{equation}
\end{revadded}
\revreplace{Relation evidence is aggregated by maxima over incoming and outgoing edges to limit degree bias from highly connected nodes.  Bounded \oc{} and pair-ledger bonuses prioritize severe recurring hypotheses, allowing persistent relation evidence to outweigh isolated local judgments.}{Here, $e_T(v)$ is the mean normalized evidence score, $\mathcal{R}_T(v)$ contains qualifying observed pairs involving $v$, and $r_T(p)$ is their pair score. We set $b_T(v)=0$ when no pair qualifies. $O_T$ denotes \oc{}-flagged nodes. Critical-pair evidence contributes through $e_T(v)$ and protected-pair seeding. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_formula_details} specifies the calculation details.}

\subsection{\revreplace{Online conformal-style evidence signal}{Online evidence-discovery signal}}

Single LLM judgments are noisy, especially on local windows. \revreplace{We therefore add a calibration-inspired discovery signal. At each rollout, \samcgs{} compares the strongest local relation-conflict score against an online quantile of previous conflict scores. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_formula_details} gives the exact rule. We call a threshold crossing an online calibrated conflict signal (\oc{}). The signal marks a local window whose ordered cycle or contradiction cue is unusually strong relative to earlier rollouts, and endpoints of the implicated relation receive an \oc{} bonus in Eq.~\ref{eq:priority}. The calibration motivation follows conformal calibration \mbox{\citep{vovk2005algorithmic,gibbs2021adaptive}}. No formal coverage guarantee is claimed because the LLM evaluator and rollout policy are adaptive. We use \oc{} as evidence routing and convergence support, while final evaluation is still based on the returned subgraph $H$.}{We therefore compute online conflict (\oc{}) flags from accumulated node-score histories. Once sufficient evidence is available, a node is flagged when at least two of three criteria exceed their thresholds: standardized mean risk, average empirical percentile, and the fraction of scores above the pooled median. Flagged nodes remain in \oc{} memory, influence subsequent search priorities, and receive the bounded bonus in Eq.~\ref{eq:priority}. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_formula_details} specifies the statistics, eligibility conditions, and thresholds. No formal coverage guarantee is claimed. Final evaluation remains based on the returned subgraph $H$.}

\subsection{Dynamic core compression}
\label{sec:dynamic-core}

\revdel{We maintain the final risk subgraph as a dynamic core with replacement, avoiding monotonic expansion.  Classical greedy guarantees apply to monotone submodular objectives under cardinality constraints \mbox{\citep{nemhauser1978analysis}}. We use greedy replacement as a heuristic for the relation-aware utility below. After each rollout, \samcgs{} forms a candidate pool $C_t$ from the local risk subgraph, the previous core, \oc{} endpoints, and top-scoring relation endpoints with local neighbors. The core is selected by maximizing a bounded utility:} \revreplace{We form the final risk subgraph as a dynamic core, avoiding a monotone union of all suspicious records. After the rollout budget is exhausted, \samcgs{} constructs the final core from the accumulated node and pair evidence under the component-specific cap. We express the desired balance between evidence retention and compactness through the following selection objective:}{After the rollout budget, the pool $C_T^\circ=V_T^{\mathrm{ev}}\cup O_T\cup\operatorname{End}(\mathcal Q_T)$ combines evidence-bearing nodes, \oc{} flags, and endpoints of supported pairs, including nonedges. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_formula_details} defines eligibility and the empty-pool fallback $C_T$. The conceptual target is:}
\ifrevisionmarks
\[
    \begin{aligned}
    &\revdeletedmath{H_t = \arg\max_{\substack{H\subseteq C_t\\ |H|\leq B(S)}}\Bigg[\sum_{v\in H}s_t(v)}\\
    &\revdeletedmath{\quad + \eta\sum_{(u,v)\in E(H)}m_t(u,v)}\\
    &\revdeletedmath{\quad - \lambda |H|\Bigg].}
    \end{aligned}
\]
\fi
\begin{revadded}
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    H_T^\star &\in \arg\max_{\substack{H\subseteq C_T\\ |H|\leq B(S)}}
    \biggl[\sum_{v\in H}s_T(v)\\
    &\quad + \eta_p \sum_{\substack{p\subseteq H\\|p|=2}}m_T(p)
    - \lambda_c |H|\biggr].
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:core}
\end{equation}
\end{revadded}
\revreplace{We implement this objective with greedy replacement: high-confidence endpoints may evict weaker nodes, whereas repeatedly supported relation endpoints are retained as locked evidence.}{We construct the returned core $H_T$ heuristically by seeding protected and strong pairs, scanning nodes in priority order with score- and gap-based stopping, and adding supported counterparts within the cap.}  \revreplace{This design aims to retain critical evidence under a fixed core budget. When a rollout observes both endpoints of a severe contradiction, subsequent rollouts should revisit and stabilize that relation, instead of allowing unrelated high-scoring distractors to occupy the subgraph.}{Equation~\ref{eq:core} counts each unordered pair once; unobserved pairs have zero support. The conceptual pair reward $\eta_p$ and size penalty $\lambda_c$ are distinct from $\lambda_s$ and $\lambda_p$.} The paired replay in Section~\ref{sec:component_tests} isolates the effect of final-core replacement using identical search trajectories.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\small
\caption{\samcgs{} for one strongly connected component}
\label{alg:samcgs}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE \revreplace{Component $S=(V_S,E_S)$}{Component $G_S=(S,E_S)$}, budget $T$, \revadd{window capacity $w$,} shared rubric and domain serializer
\STATE Initialize memory $M_0$\revdel{ and core $H_0=\emptyset$}
\FOR{$t=1$ to $T$}
    \ifrevisionmarks
    \STATE \revdel{Select seed $a_t=\arg\max_{v\in V_S}U_t(v)$ using Eq.~\ref{eq:ucb}}
    \STATE \revdel{Build local window $W_t$ around $a_t$ plus memory bridges}
    \fi
    \STATE \revadd{Select a capacity-limited window $W_t$ using Eq.~\ref{eq:window-capacity} and the local-window policy}
    \STATE \revreplace{Query LLM with the shared risk rubric on $W_t$}{Reuse an eligible cached evaluation; otherwise query the LLM on $W_t$ with the shared rubric}
    \STATE \revreplace{Receive $y_t(v)$, $z_t(u,v)$, local subgraph $h_t$, and \oc{} evidence}{Receive evaluation entry $\mathcal E_t$ with scores, pair reports, and local subgraph $h_t$}
    \STATE Update node and relation memory $M_t$
    \STATE \revadd{Update \oc{} flags from accumulated node-score histories}
    \ifrevisionmarks
    \STATE \revdel{Recompute dynamic core $H_t$ using Eq.~\ref{eq:core}}
    \fi
\ENDFOR
\STATE \revadd{Select final core $H_T$ from accumulated memory using Algorithm~\ref{alg:appendix_dynamic_core}}
\RETURN \revreplace{risk subgraph $H_T$ and node ranking by $s_T(v)$}{$H_T$ and node ranking by mean observed risk $\bar q_T(v)$}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Computational feasibility}

\samcgs{} is an anytime graph-search procedure in the sense of
\citet{zilberstein1996anytime}. Tarjan preprocessing is $O(|V|+|E|)$.
\revreplace{For a component $S$, the method performs $T$ local LLM evaluations over windows of at most $w$ records, with prompt cost $O(T\cdot\operatorname{tokens}(w))$. Its evidence memory is keyed by physical nodes and observed relations, not path-copy states, and stores $O(|S|+|E_S|)$ statistics. The dynamic core cap $B(S)$ bounds the retained subgraph, allowing additional budget to refine the same physical memory without expanding a cyclic tree indefinitely. The resulting collapsed evaluation point is anytime-compatible for higher-level graph search.}{For each component, $T$ rollout slots use windows of at most $w$ records; eligible cache hits reuse evaluations. Input-token workload is $\sum_{j=1}^{C}L_j$ for $C$ executed model requests with serialized prompt lengths $L_j$. Basic node and graph-edge statistics occupy $O(|S|+|E_S|)$ space; caches, score histories, pair ledgers, and optional traces require additional storage. The cap $B(S)$ bounds the returned core. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_formula_details} specifies accounting and storage.}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{LLM reasoning and long context.}
Chain-of-thought prompting supports sequential reasoning \citep{wei2022chain}; self-consistency aggregates answers across sampled reasoning paths \citep{wang2023selfconsistency}. Long-context and graph studies identify retrieval and encoding limitations \citep{liu2024lost,hsieh2024ruler,bai2024longbench,wang2023can,fatemi2024talk}. Legal benchmarks cover contract extraction and inference \citep{hendrycks2021cuad,koreeda2021contractnli}, mergers \citep{wang2023maud}, provision classification \citep{tuggener2020ledgar}, unfair clauses \citep{lippi2019claudette}, and broader legal understanding \citep{chalkidis2022lexglue,guha2023legalbench}. Legal networks and contract graphs model record relationships \citep{boulet2021measuring,dechtiar2025graphgrpolex}.

\paragraph{Search-augmented LLMs.}
Tree of Thoughts \citep{yao2024tree}, Graph of Thoughts \citep{besta2024graph}, and reasoning as planning \citep{hao2023reasoning} organize reasoning through search. ReAct interleaves reasoning and actions \citep{yao2023react}; LATS combines MCTS with LLM evaluation, reflection, and environmental feedback \citep{zhou2024lats}.

\paragraph{Monte Carlo search on graphs.}
Monte Carlo graph search shares transpositions \citep{childs2008transpositions,czech2021monte}, while related analyses address loop-induced uncertainty \citep{moerland2020mcts}. ReKG-MCTS \citep{song2025rekgmcts} and Graph-MCTS \citep{liu2025graphmcts} apply MCTS to reasoning over external graph knowledge. SubgraphX uses MCTS to identify subgraphs that explain GNN predictions \citep{yuan2021subgraphx}.

\paragraph{Graph retrieval and evidence selection.}
RAG conditions generation on retrieved passages \citep{lewis2020rag}; IRCoT interleaves retrieval and reasoning \citep{trivedi2023interleaving}. GraphRAG summarizes communities \citep{edge2024graphrag}, ToG-2 couples graph traversal and document retrieval \citep{ma2025tog2}, and G-Retriever selects query-relevant subgraphs through prize-collecting Steiner tree optimization \citep{he2024gretriever}. LegalGraphRAG combines hierarchical legal graphs, retrieval, and verification \citep{chen2026legalgraphrag}; SAT-Graph RAG models legal hierarchy, time, and provenance \citep{demartim2025satgraph}. \samcgs{} accumulates relation evidence within \scc{}s to retain risk endpoints in compact subgraphs.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced \samcgs{}, a structure-aware Monte Carlo graph search method for cyclic document risk. \revadd{It accumulates node and pair evidence within SCCs across local windows, then selects a bounded core after search.}

Across four domains and five LLMs, \samcgs{} achieves higher mean \rootthree{} and \riskall{} than one-shot prompting and LEA. \revadd{Shared-trace replay further shows that final-core replacement improves endpoint retention at the same mean core size. Expert evaluation supports evidence quality on injected variants and case-level risk detection on unmodified sources.} Its compact risk subgraphs consolidate cyclic dependencies into evidence for human inspection.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\revadd{\samcgs{} focuses on risk-evidence extraction within cyclic document components. Its local LLM search incurs additional inference cost, making high-risk components a natural priority for deployment. Our evaluation spans four domains and combines controlled injection with expert assessment of 18 unmodified sources; broader natural-case coverage and end-to-end document review are directions for further evaluation. Performance depends on the backbone and graph construction, particularly for no-risk recognition and evidence completeness in long contracts. \relationmemorymark{\stageoneupdate{Stage-1 component comparisons cover 80 variants from 20 source SCCs on one backbone. Prior sensitivity covers four sources; broader source coverage and parameter settings would provide a more complete assessment of robustness.}}}

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
\label{sec:ethics}

\revadd{\samcgs{} is intended to support human review by identifying candidate risk evidence in cyclic document components. Its outputs should be checked against the original documents by qualified reviewers and should not be used as the sole basis for legal, regulatory, or other consequential decisions.}

\revadd{The conflicts introduced through controlled injection are constructed evaluation cases. They should not be interpreted as factual claims about the organizations or individuals named in the source documents, or as evidence that the original documents contain those conflicts.}

\section{Diagnostic Metrics and Ablation Definitions}
\label{app:diagnostics}

The main paper evaluates \rootthree{}, \riskany{}, \riskall{}, and
compression.  We also log two diagnostics.  \textbf{Effective
compression} is computed only on cases whose returned subgraph retains at
least one repair-relevant endpoint.  It separates useful compression from
trivial compression that drops all evidence.  \textbf{Effective \oc{}}
\revreplace{counts an \oc{} discovery only when the implicated relation intersects the repair-relevant risk region. It is useful for convergence analysis, although it is cumulative by construction and is therefore not a primary success metric.}{at a rollout prefix indicates whether accumulated \oc{}-flagged nodes intersect the injected risk or affected-record set. Its rate averages this cumulative indicator over cases with an available trace point at or before that prefix. It is a convergence diagnostic, not a primary success metric.}

\section{Expert Annotation and Evaluation}
\label{sec:appendix_annotation}

We report the original 12-case audit below and the new 42-presentation audit in Section~\ref{app:expert_extension} separately because their annotation schemes and annotator coverage differ. The original aggregate results are retained without imputing missing labels.
\subsection{Original 12-case audit}

We construct a representative expert-audit set to verify that the critical structural risks used in our benchmark are recognizable to qualified domain readers, not artifacts of hidden system labels.  The audit is \emph{system-blind}.  Annotators see only paragraph text, paragraph identifiers, and ordinary natural-language instructions.  They are not shown model outputs, hidden node roles, system suggestions, \samcgs{} cores, baseline subgraphs, or terms such as root, witness, affected node, \scc{}, and risk subgraph.  Hidden fields are used only after annotation to compare expert labels with injected endpoints and system outputs.

All three annotators hold doctoral degrees in relevant fields and have professional experience with legal, regulatory, or contractual analysis. Table~\ref{tab:expert_protocol} summarizes the annotation tasks.

\paragraph{Case-level task.}
For each case, the expert reads a group of related paragraphs and answers whether the group contains a structural conflict.  A structural conflict means that two or more paragraphs cannot all be satisfied together, leave a high-impact condition unresolved, or make a repair decision depend on inconsistent assumptions.

\paragraph{Paragraph-level task.}
For each paragraph, the expert chooses one of four labels:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Clear issue}: the paragraph is itself problematic, or clearly conflicts with another visible paragraph.
    \item \textbf{Possibly relevant}: the paragraph may be needed to understand or repair the conflict, but is not clearly wrong by itself.
    \item \textbf{No issue observed}: the paragraph appears to be ordinary context.
    \item \textbf{Uncertain}: the expert cannot decide without more context or specialist knowledge.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Repair-material task.}
After labeling paragraphs, the expert selects which paragraphs should be included in the final repair packet.  This task is stricter than identifying a suspicious sentence because the packet should include enough material for a reviewer to understand and fix the conflict.

\paragraph{Annotation protocol.}
Each sample is annotated independently by three experts.  Every sample therefore receives three case-level decisions, three sets of paragraph-level labels, and three repair-packet selections.  Disagreements are preserved for analysis without being forced into an immediate majority vote.

\paragraph{Audit results.}
Table~\ref{tab:expert_audit_results} summarizes the completed annotations.  All 12 audited cases receive a majority structural-conflict label.  Experts also recognize the injected evidence endpoints.  In total, 23 of 24 root/witness endpoint paragraphs receive a majority \emph{clear issue} label, and all 24 receive a majority \emph{clear issue} or \emph{possibly relevant} label.  Agreement is strongest for the clear-risk endpoint decision, with Fleiss' $\kappa=0.606$ and pairwise Cohen's $\kappa$ ranging from 0.452 to 0.759.  These results support that the injected critical risks are semantically visible to qualified readers when the relevant paragraphs are inspected.

The repair-packet task is intentionally stricter and more variable.  One annotator selected no individual paragraph for the final packet while still marking every case as conflicting and most cases as sufficiently or partially supported.  We therefore treat repair-packet selection as a conservative diagnostic of what a reviewer would include for remediation, not as the primary evidence that a conflict exists.

\section{Core Algorithm Details}
\label{sec:appendix_algorithm}

Algorithms~\ref{alg:appendix_scc_localized}--\ref{alg:appendix_dynamic_core} expand the overview in Algorithm~\ref{alg:samcgs} into detailed pseudocode.  The implementation uses the same high-level objects as the main paper, including a document graph $G$, strongly connected components $S$, local rollout windows $W_t$, relation-first memory $M_t$, \revreplace{online calibrated conflict signals}{\oc{} node flags computed from accumulated score histories}, and a final dynamic core $H_T$.

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\small
\caption{\scc{}-localized search}
\label{alg:appendix_scc_localized}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Document graph $G=(V,E)$, rollout budget $T$, shared risk rubric
\STATE $\mathcal{S}\leftarrow$ Tarjan strongly connected components of $G$
\STATE Initialize collapsed graph $G' \leftarrow G$
\FORALL{component $S\in\mathcal{S}$}
    \IF{$|S|=1$}
        \STATE Keep the singleton record unchanged
    \ELSE
        \STATE Build local search unit $G_S=(S,E_S)$
        \STATE $(H_S,r_S)\leftarrow \textsc{SA-MCGS-Rollout}(G_S,T)$ \revadd{($r_S$: mean-risk ranking)}
        \STATE Replace $S$ in $G'$ with collapsed evaluation point $(H_S,r_S)$
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\RETURN Collapsed graph $G'$ and all component risk subgraphs
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\small
\caption{\samcgs{} rollout inside one component}
\label{alg:appendix_rollout}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Component $G_S=(S,E_S)$, budget $T$, \revadd{window capacity $w$,} shared rubric
\STATE \revreplace{Initialize node evidence, relation evidence, \oc{} evidence, and core $H_0=\emptyset$}{Initialize memory $M_0$ with node evidence, pair ledger, and \oc{} flags}
\FOR{$t=1$ to $T$}
    \ifrevisionmarks
    \STATE \revdel{Select a seed record using SCC-aware UCB with relation priors}
    \STATE \revdel{Construct local window $W_t$ from the seed, nearby edges, and memory bridges}
    \fi
    \STATE \revadd{Select $W_t$ with capacity $w$ by frontier expansion or an eligible scheduled endpoint probe}
    \STATE \revreplace{Query the LLM with the shared risk rubric on $W_t$}{Reuse an eligible cached evaluation; otherwise query the LLM on $W_t$ with the shared rubric}
    \STATE \revreplace{Parse local risky records, relation evidence, and optional \oc{} signal}{Parse consumed entry $\mathcal E_t$: node scores, pair reports, and local subgraph}
    \STATE \revreplace{Update node risk scores, pair evidence, witness-style evidence, and affected-region evidence}{Update node and pair memory using Algorithm~\ref{alg:appendix_relation_memory}}
    \STATE \revadd{Update \oc{} flags from accumulated node-score histories}
    \ifrevisionmarks
    \STATE \revdel{$H_t\leftarrow\!\textsc{Dynamic-Core-Compression}(H_{t-1},M_t)$}
    \fi
\ENDFOR

\RETURN \revreplace{Final core $H_T$ and ranking induced by accumulated evidence}{$H_T$ and node ranking by mean observed risk $\bar q_T(v)$}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\small
\caption{Relation-first evidence memory}
\label{alg:appendix_relation_memory}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE \revreplace{Rollout result $R_t$, previous memory $M_{t-1}$}{Window $W_t$, consumed entry $\mathcal E_t$, previous memory $M_{t-1}$}
\STATE $M_t\leftarrow M_{t-1}$

\ENDFOR

    \fi
\ENDFOR

\STATE \revadd{Update local-subgraph and repair-entry counts from their entry-level lists, with their node and path evidence}
\STATE \revadd{Update window-fragment evidence and directed graph-edge visit/conflict statistics within $W_t$}
\ifrevisionmarks
\STATE \revdel{\textbf{if} $R_t$ contains an \oc{} signal \textbf{then}}

\fi
\RETURN $M_t$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\small
\caption{Dynamic core compression}
\label{alg:appendix_dynamic_core}
\ifrevisionmarks

\STATE \revdel{\textbf{end for}}

\STATE \revdel{\textbf{end while}}

\STATE \revdel{\textbf{end while}}

\end{algorithmic}

    \RETURN First node of $C_T$ in SCC order, or $\emptyset$ if $C_T=\emptyset$

    \STATE $H_T\leftarrow H_T\cup\{v\}$
\ENDFOR
\STATE Add supported counterparts satisfying the pair-closure rule within $B(S)$
\RETURN $H_T$ in SCC order
\end{algorithmic}
\endgroup
\end{algorithm}

The core is therefore not a monotone union of all suspicious records.  \revreplace{Later rollouts can replace weak records with stronger evidence, although the update cannot drop repeatedly supported critical endpoints only to obtain a smaller subgraph.}{Evidence from later rollouts can change the final selection, with protected-pair seeding prioritizing repeatedly supported endpoints under the cap.}

\section{Formula Details for Evidence Updates}
\label{sec:appendix_formula_details}

Implementation-level equations summarized in Section~\ref{sec:method} are expanded here.  The main text retains the search template plus two scoring components, the priority score and core objective.  Remaining details were fixed before evaluation and left untuned on test cases.

\begin{revadded}
\paragraph{Window selection and evaluation reuse.}
Window capacity $w$ is independent of graph radius; the reported local-window experiments use $w=4$. On an eligible scheduled probe, the selector first considers a critical-pair revisit, then a supported relation pair. Scheduling uses configured warm-up and periodic gates; critical revisits also obey their own gate. If no probe qualifies, the selector chooses a UCB seed and expands the incoming/outgoing adjacency frontier under Eq.~\ref{eq:ucb}. An empty frontier permits at most one randomly chosen remaining node before stopping. A probe includes its endpoints and a shortest path in the undirected projection when the path fits the capacity; otherwise it starts from the endpoints. Remaining positions are filled from neighbors ranked by edge and node priors, then by component-wide node evidence, always respecting $w$. Windows need not be full or connected, and displayed graph edges retain their original directions.

Cache lookup uses the unordered window-node set. An existing entry is reused while its reuse counter is below \texttt{tt\_max\_reuse}; otherwise the evaluator is invoked. Reused entries still update memory. The search counter \texttt{llm\_calls} counts returned non-cache evaluations, including caught failures that yield default scores, rather than underlying API attempts. Retries and restarted executions can add requests, so recorded call/token usage follows the logs and recovery accounting in Appendix~\ref{app:revision_cost}. Each $L_j$ includes the serialized record text, visible edges, and prompt template; a fixed record count does not fix token length. Beyond scalar node/graph-edge statistics, cached outputs, accumulated score histories, observed-pair records, and retained traces occupy storage that grows with the saved observations. The returned-core cap does not bound this storage.

\paragraph{Exploration-score implementation.}
Algorithms and schematics use serial notation; concurrent workers select from launch-time snapshots and update memory in completion order. All terms below refer to the selection-time snapshot. Let $A(x)$ denote the first three terms of Eq.~\ref{eq:ucb}. The implementation mixes this score with normalized Gamma noise as $(1-d)A(x)+dg\xi(x)$. For $0\le d<1$, dividing finite candidate scores by the common positive factor $1-d$ preserves the selected maximizer and yields
\[
    \eta(x)=\frac{dg}{1-d}\xi(x).
\]
Gamma draws use the configured shape and unit scale, divided by their sum plus $10^{-8}$. The scale is $g=10$ for seeds and $g=5$ for frontier edges. This mapping preserves selection, not the numerical mixed scores. For seeds, $N$ is the total node visit count; for frontier expansion it is the sum over the current candidate edges, both floored at one. The term $\ell$ includes the configured virtual-loss weight. A seed with $n+\ell=0$ receives infinite priority; a frontier edge uses that branch only if its statistics are absent. Multiple infinite scores are resolved by candidate order. Progressive bias uses actual visits, with $\lambda_p=\texttt{relation\_prior\_weight}$ and $\delta=\texttt{relation\_prior\_decay}$; the exploration denominator uses $\epsilon=10^{-8}$.
\end{revadded}

\begin{revadded}
\paragraph{Core priority.}
Equation~\ref{eq:priority} uses $\alpha=0.24$ and $\gamma=0.16$. Each nonnegative evidence channel is divided by its maximum within the SCC. Channels whose maximum exceeds $10^{-12}$ form a shared averaging set for all nodes; missing entries contribute zero, and $e_T(v)=0$ if this set is empty. The channels measure risk above the median over visited nodes, conflict rate, local-subgraph appearances, repair-entry support, conflict-pair endpoint counts, \oc{} flags, AMAF evidence, path-fragment evidence, relation-prior support, and critical-pair endpoint support. The last channel sums retained critical-pair scores incident to the node, multiplying locked entries by $1.25$.

For a locally supported pair $p=\{u,v\}$, the initial score is
\[
\begin{aligned}
r_T^0(p)={}&0.45\sigma(p)+0.20\widehat{x}_T(p)+0.20\widehat{m}_T(p)\\
&+0.15[e_T(u)+e_T(v)]/2,
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $\sigma$ is semantic conflict strength; hats denote log-normalized explicit-report counts $x_T(p)$ and pair support $m_T(p)$. Each count is transformed by $\log(1+\mathrm{count})$ and divided by the maximum transformed count of its type across local pairs, with zero used when that maximum is zero. A retained critical-pair entry ($\kappa_T(p)\ge\tau_{\mathrm{exit}}$) sets $r_T(p)=\max\{r_T^0(p),\kappa_T(p)\}$, taking a missing local score as zero; otherwise $r_T=r_T^0$.

\revreplace{A pair with both endpoints in the SCC qualifies if $r_T(p)\ge\tau_{\mathrm{pair}}$, its semantic strength is at least $0.65$, it has an explicit report, or it is protected. Merged records use the maximum local and retained critical-pair semantic strength. Protection eligibility means a locked ledger state or $\kappa_T(p)\ge\tau_{\mathrm{core}}$ with at least $L$ supports. The default thresholds are $\tau_{\mathrm{pair}}=0.42$, $\tau_{\mathrm{exit}}=0.48$, $\tau_{\mathrm{core}}=0.62$, and $L=2$. Thus $\mathcal{R}_T(v)$ contains qualifying pairs incident to $v$, with $b_T(v)=0$ for an empty set. $O_T$ is the persistent set of \oc{}-flagged nodes.}{Let $\mathcal Q_T$ contain merged observed pairs with both endpoints in $S$ satisfying at least one condition: $r_T(p)\ge\tau_{\mathrm{pair}}$, semantic strength at least $\tau_{\mathrm{sem}}$, or a positive explicit-report count. Merged records use the maximum local and retained critical-pair semantic strength. For Eq.~\ref{eq:priority}, $\mathcal R_T(v)$ contains incident pairs in $\mathcal Q_T$ and incident protected pairs. Protection eligibility means a locked ledger state or $\kappa_T(p)\ge\tau_{\mathrm{core}}$ with at least $L$ supports. Defaults are $\tau_{\mathrm{pair}}=0.42$, $\tau_{\mathrm{sem}}=0.65$, $\tau_{\mathrm{exit}}=0.48$, $\tau_{\mathrm{core}}=0.62$, and $L=2$. $O_T$ is the persistent set of \oc{}-flagged nodes.}
\end{revadded}

\revreplace{For every record observed in rollout $t$}{For every record in the consumed window $W_t$}, node risk evidence is updated by a visit-counted running average:

\begin{aligned}

\fi

    \begin{aligned}
    n_t(v)&=n_{t-1}(v)+1,\\
    \bar q_t(v)&=\frac{n_{t-1}(v)\bar q_{t-1}(v)+y_t(v)}{n_t(v)}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:node-update}
\end{equation}
\end{revadded}
\revdel{Directed relation evidence is updated by exponential smoothing,}
\ifrevisionmarks

    \revdeletedmath{m_{t+1}(u,v)=(1-\rho)m_t(u,v)+\rho z_t(u,v),}

\fi
\revdel{where $z_t(u,v)$ is the local evaluator's evidence that relation $(u,v)$ creates or supports a repair-relevant conflict.}
\begin{revadded}
Index $t$ counts consumed entries $\mathcal E_t$ in completion order, including cache reuse; missing node scores default to $0.5$. For an unordered pair $p=\{u,v\}$ of distinct SCC nodes, let $\mathcal U_t$ be the set of valid pairs appearing in the entry's \texttt{conflicts} or \texttt{local\_conflict\_edges}. Let $k_t^{\mathrm{exp}}(p)$ count valid reports for $p$ in \texttt{conflicts}. Local support $m_t(p)$ and explicit-report count $x_t(p)$ start at zero and accumulate as
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    m_t(p)&=m_{t-1}(p)+\mathbf{1}[p\in\mathcal U_t],\\
    x_t(p)&=x_{t-1}(p)+k_t^{\mathrm{exp}}(p).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:relation-update}
\end{equation}
Thus local support increases at most once per pair per consumed entry, whereas explicit counts follow report multiplicity; a local edge alone is not an explicit report. Relation reasons are retained separately. Reports for each pair are merged before one critical-ledger update per entry, retaining their explicit/local report counts. These counts describe consumed evidence, not independent model calls.

Endpoint and directional AMAF/path increments remain report-specific; deduplicating pair support does not deduplicate those increments. Local-subgraph and repair-entry counts are updated from the entry's node lists outside the relation loop. Window-fragment evidence uses the ordered window and the entry's context nodes. Directed graph edges with both endpoints in the window receive separate visits; their conflict indicator is one when both endpoints occur in the entry's explicit-conflict node set. Algorithm~\ref{alg:appendix_relation_memory} summarizes these updates; Algorithms~\ref{alg:samcgs} and~\ref{alg:appendix_rollout} then compute \oc{} flags from the accumulated node histories.
\end{revadded}

\paragraph{Relation priors and critical pairs.}
\revreplace{Equation~\ref{eq:relation-prior} is shared by node and edge priors. For nodes we set $w=\theta$ and use node features such as repair-entry support, local-subgraph appearance, pair-endpoint support, and}{Equation~\ref{eq:relation-prior} summarizes base normalization. Nodes use $\mathbf w=\theta$ with repair-entry support, local-subgraph appearance, pair-endpoint support, and} \revreplace{\oc{} endpoint evidence}{\oc{} node flags}. \revreplace{For edges we set $w=\beta$ and use edge features such as semantic incompatibility, repeated pair support, reverse-edge evidence, and accumulated conflict evidence.}{Edges use $\mathbf w=\beta$ with critical-pair score, local semantic strength, explicit reports, pair support, directional AMAF, and accumulated conflict evidence.}
\begin{revadded}
Time-indexed priors use completed-evidence snapshot $M_t$; Eq.~\ref{eq:ucb} reads the current selection-time snapshot. For exact normalization, let $a_t^V$ and $a_t^E$ be the nonnegative weighted node and edge scores, and $M_t^V$, $M_t^E$ their maxima. The node maximum ranges over $S$; the edge maximum ranges over the original directed edges and their reverse keys. The implementation returns zero when the relevant maximum is at most $\epsilon_\pi=10^{-12}$; otherwise it divides by that maximum without the additive $\epsilon$ in the compact template. Thus node prior $\pi_t(v)=a_t^V(v)/M_t^V$ when $M_t^V>\epsilon_\pi$. For a frontier edge $e$ and its endpoint $v_{\mathrm{new}}$ outside the current window $W$, the positive-maximum branch is
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_t^E(e)&=a_t^E(e)/M_t^E,\quad M_t^E>\epsilon_\pi,\\
\pi_t(e;W)&=\min\{1,b_t^E(e)\\
&\qquad+\beta_{\mathrm{enter}}\pi_t(v_{\mathrm{new}})\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $\beta_{\mathrm{enter}}=0.25$; no node bonus is added in the zero-maximum branch. \relationmemorymark{Disabling relation-first selection sets both priors to zero.} In Table~\ref{tab:prior_coefficients}, $\beta_{\mathrm{critical}}=1.55$ multiplies the full critical-pair score, without final-core eligibility filtering, and $\beta_{\mathrm{semantic}}=1.35$ multiplies semantic strength from local pair reasons. The reverse-AMAF feature is first scaled by $0.55$, then multiplied by $\beta_{\mathrm{reverse}}=0.35$.
\end{revadded}
A separate bounded ledger records persistent critical-pair hypotheses:
\ifrevisionmarks
\[
    \begin{aligned}

\fi

\end{equation}
for $\{u,v\}\in\mathcal L_t$, the unordered node pairs recorded in the ledger after consuming entry $\mathcal E_t$.
\end{revadded}
Here $\chi_t(u,v)$ contains pair-level evidence such as semantic incompatibility, severity, same-path support, no-fallback language, repeated support, and explicit conflict reports.
\begin{revadded}

\section{Shared Risk Rubric Prompt}
\label{sec:appendix_shared_rubric}

Reviewer fairness depends on separating the risk definition from the search mechanism.  The following excerpt gives the shared evaluator rubric used by one-shot prompting and the local-window evaluators used by LEA and \samcgs{}.

\begin{quote}
\small
You are given records from a directed graph of interdependent documents.  Identify records or record sets that are structurally inconsistent, mutually incompatible, underspecified, or high-impact if left unresolved.  Use only visible record text and visible directed relations.  Do not assume a domain-specific defect type.  Return evidence-based judgments with node ids and concise reasons.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Shared semantics.}
One-shot prompting, LEA, and \samcgs{} share this risk rubric. One-shot produces a global ranking and subgraph in one call; LEA aggregates fixed local evaluations; \samcgs{} adaptively acquires local evidence to construct its final core.

\paragraph{Formatting interface.}
Record formats vary by domain: Debian records contain package fields, BGB records are statutory sections, SEC EX-21 records are subsidiary disclosures, and CUAD records are contract clauses. Lightweight domain formatters
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{A}_d=(\operatorname{serialize}_d,\operatorname{edges}_d,\operatorname{normalize}_d)
\end{equation}
serialize records, expose typed directed edges, and normalize model outputs without specifying a target risk type or revealing injected endpoints. All three methods apply the same domain-neutral rubric to visible node text and directed edges, identifying structural inconsistency, mutual incompatibility, underspecification, and high-impact risks. They differ in how they acquire and aggregate evidence: global generation, fixed local aggregation, or adaptive \scc{}-aware search.



Table~\ref{tab:dataset_processing} summarizes the four included domains and their conversion into directed record graphs.  Domain coverage spans several forms of authority and structure, including technical dependency metadata, public corporate disclosures, official statutory references, and expert-labeled contract clauses.

\paragraph{SCC construction and filtering.}
For each domain, we construct a directed record graph and enumerate non-trivial \scc{}s using Tarjan's algorithm.  Candidate components are filtered by size and record availability and then locked before model evaluation.  The main experiment uses the same locked \scc{} blocks for One-shot, LEA, and \samcgs{} under the same model and profile.



\paragraph{Injection constraints.}
The critical benchmark modifies only existing records in the selected \scc{}.  No new nodes are added.  The injected root record provides the endpoint used for \rootthree{} evaluation.  A distant witness record supplies the evidence that makes the root-side statement structurally incompatible under the graph context.  Affected records are downstream or neighboring records whose interpretation changes under the root--witness conflict.  Subgraph metrics count root, witness, and affected records as repair-relevant endpoints when applicable. Table~\ref{tab:injection_templates} lists the four injection templates.

\paragraph{Why \riskall{} matters.}
\riskany{} is useful when a repair process can sometimes start from one good entry point.  \riskall{} is stricter and central for critical cases, where fixing a structural contradiction often requires both sides of the incompatibility.  A subgraph that includes only the obvious-risk paragraph but omits the distant condition may be too small to support a reliable repair.
